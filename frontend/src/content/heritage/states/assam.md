## History & Origin

Assam holds a singular chapter in global textile history as the exclusive natural home of **Muga silk** (*Antheraea assamensis*)—a golden wild silkworm nourished on aromatic Som and Soalu leaves along the Brahmaputra Valley for millennia. Ancient epics and 7th-century royal gifts praised Assam's shimmering yellow cloth, which astonishingly grows *more* radiant after gentle washing.

Under Ahom royal patronage, **Sualkuchi** developed into "the weaving capital of the East," where every household kept wooden *tat-xal* looms crafting both traditional **Mekhela Chador** sets and classic six-yard silk sarees.

## Regional Craft & Weaving Centers

**Sualkuchi** on the northern bank of the Brahmaputra leads Assam's heritage silk production—uniting warm honey-toned **Muga**, gleaming pearl-white **Assam Pat**, and soft cloud-warm **Eri** (Ahimsa peace silk).

Signature border styles draw from historical *Kinkhwab* brocades, crisp *miri* zigzags, geometric *japi* sun hats, and stylized Assam tea and orchid motifs.

## Material Characteristics & Drape

Muga yarn possesses natural tensile strength paired with an unmistakable burnished antique-gold sheen that catches warm light cleanly without needing metallic zari thread to shimmer.

Assam *Pat* mulberry silk delivers a crisp ivory luminosity prized for contrast borders in jewel crimson, deep teal, or black, draping smoothly with clean pleats.

## Craftsmanship & Loom Technique

On the traditional wooden Assamese handloom (*tat-xal*), artisans deploy an extra-weft patterning technique using tiny bamboo shuttles (*maku*) to insert raised crimson, emerald, or gold geometric motifs (*phool*) cleanly on the obverse face.

Because Muga filaments resist uniform mechanical speed, patient hand-reeling and calm pit-loom tension ensure even thickness and heirloom durability.

## Cultural Significance

Central to Bohag Bihu dances, Assamese bridal blessings, and grand festive gatherings across India, a golden Muga or pearl Pat saree glows like sunlit heirloom gold worn effortlessly across generations.

## Care & Preservation Guidance

- Dry-clean intricate Kinkhwab-patterned Muga and Pat silk sarees; pure unembellished Muga may be dry-cleaned or gently hand-rinsed in cool water only with specialist mild silk soap.
- Dry carefully flat in shaded breezy air—never wring golden wild silk.
- Iron gently on reverse at low silk temperature beneath a light cotton cloth.
- Store inside clean breathable cotton wraps away from harsh sunlight.
