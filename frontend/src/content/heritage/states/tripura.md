## History & Origin

For centuries across Tripura's fertile hills, women of the Debbarma, Jamatia, and Reang communities wove the iconic **Rignai** (lower wrap) and **Risa** chest cloth—encoding folk legends, bamboo weaves, and river bends in crisp stepped geometry on portable bamboo looms.

Today, cooperative handloom artisans around **Agartala, Khowai, and Udaipur** weave that unmistakable **Rignai border aesthetic** across airy combed-cotton, bamboo-cotton, and tussar-silk full sarees celebrated throughout Northeast and mainland India.

## Regional Craft & Weaving Centers

**Agartala, Khowai, and Dhalai** anchor Tripura's cooperative looms—preserving over a hundred heirloom border patterns including *Toisa* (river bend), *Khamchwi* (royal lotus), and *Muiya* bamboo-shoot chevrons set brilliantly against ivory, jewel maroon, bottle green, and black bases.

## Material Characteristics & Drape

Combed cotton and contemporary bamboo-blend Tripuri sarees offer crisp, cool breathability suited to long tropical afternoons, while raised *extra-weft* border strips lend pleasing weight that keeps the lower border swinging gracefully in motion.

## Craftsmanship & Loom Technique

Without mechanical jacquards, skilled weavers employ needle-sharp bamboo sheds and manual extra-weft counting to build intricate symmetrical diamonds and zigzag *Toisa* currents squarely inside the warp weave—often finished in vivid contrasting magenta, gold, teal, and ivory.

## Cultural Significance

Essential across *Garia Puja* harvests, seasonal spring fairs, and stylish cultural gatherings, a Rignai-inspired saree unites joyful indigenous artistry with effortless everyday silhouette.

## Care & Preservation Guidance

- Gently hand-wash pure cotton Rignai sarees separately in cool water with mild soap on early washes; dry-clean silk-blended editions.
- Never scrub hard across raised extra-weft geometric threads.
- Air dry flat in dappled shade and press smoothly from the reverse side.
- Store inside clean breathable cotton bags.
