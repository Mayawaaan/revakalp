## History & Origin

Locally honored as **Bandhakala**, Odisha's tie-resist yarn weaving tradition grew along the Mahanadi river valley where weaving guilds refined curved, organic ikat silhouettes unlike purely rigid grids elsewhere.

Classical Puri temple iconography—the *sankha* (conch), *chakra* (wheel), lotus petal, and deer—paired seamlessly with *kumbha* temple tooth borders inspired by Kalinga stone shrines.

## Regional Craft & Weaving Centers

Western Odisha's **Bargarh, Sonepur (Subarnapur), and Sambalpur** anchor Sambalpuri Ikat, famous for *Pasapalli* checkerboard borders, *Saktapar* dice patterns, and double-ikat *Bichitrapuri* masterpieces.

Farther south and east, **Bomkai** (originally Ganjam and later flourishing in Sonepur) blends subtle ikat bodies with raised extra-weft tribal symbols (*kanthi* necklace triangles and lotus buds), while **Berhampur** weaves ceremonial *Patta* silks with crisp *Phoda Kumbha* interlocking borders.

## Material Characteristics & Drape

Odisha handlooms exist prominently in both cool high-count *cotton* (celebrated for comfortable daily drape in hot coastal weather) and lustrous *mulberry or tussar silk*.

Every Sambalpuri ikat motif exhibits a characteristic soft, atmospheric "feathered" halo around its edges—caused by tiny natural alignment shifts between tied weft threads, proving genuine loom craftsmanship.

## Craftsmanship & Loom Technique

Artisans tie bundles of warp and/or weft threads according to chalk markings with rubber or cotton cords before multiple dye immersions (*bandha*). During weaving, the weaver subtly nudges each weft thread with a bamboo skewer so curved conches and creeping *lata* vines register coherently across the loom.

In **Sonepuri Bomkai** and **Nuapatna Khandua**, supplementary border harnesses build tactile geometric relief in gold, rust, and ivory.

## Cultural Significance

Odisha's classic palette of vermilion crimson, charcoal black, turmeric yellow, and ivory carries deep devotional reverence—worn proudly from coastal temples to formal cultural events nationwide.

## Care & Preservation Guidance

- Dry-clean Bomkai, Khandua, and Sambalpuri silk sarees gently without twisting.
- Wash pure cotton Sambalpuri drapes separately in cold water with mild detergent for the first few wears; avoid soaking contrasting black-and-red borders in warm water.
- Air dry evenly in cool shade to preserve deep indigo and lac crimson tones.
- Fold along relaxed lines inside breathable cotton covers.
