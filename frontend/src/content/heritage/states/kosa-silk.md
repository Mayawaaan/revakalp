## History & Origin

Wild tussar sericulture has flourished across the forested belt of eastern Central India for centuries. In Chhattisgarh, generations of Dewangan weaving families elevated sturdy forest-harvested cocoons into refined, ceremonially prized silk cloth.

Because *Antheraea mylitta* silkworms feed organically on Sal, Saja, and Arjun trees in open forests, Kosa cocoons produce thicker, stronger, naturally warm-tinted filaments than domesticated mulberry silk.

## Regional Craft & Weaving Centers

Champa in Janjgir-Champa district, along with Korba, Raigarh, and Jagdalpur in Bastar, form Chhattisgarh's primary Kosa handloom heartland. Each town is known for specialized reeling, natural vegetable dyeing, and heavy bridal textures.

Artisans frequently weave *Kosa-on-Kosa* sarees in their undyed champagne, pale honey, or toasted almond tones—allowing the natural metallic warmth of the cocoon to remain the central statement.

## Material Characteristics & Drape

Even before any dye touches the skein, Kosa carries an unmistakable biscuit, amber, and soft gold undertone. When textured with hand-drawn *ghicha* or *katia* yarns in the weft, tiny organic slubs catch ambient light across the pleats.

Though stronger and slightly crisper in handfeel than glossy mulberry weaves, Kosa molds smoothly after a few drapes and stays remarkably breathable in humid climates.

## Craftsmanship & Loom Technique

Unwinding wild tussar cocoons traditionally involves thigh-reeling (*ghicha*) or careful wet reeling so multiple strands coalesce into a continuous sturdy yarn without snapping.

Pallu designs favor earthy sophistication: bold *kumbha* temple pyramids interlocked along contrasting maroon or charcoal selvedges, *jala* geometric ribs, or folk motifs drawn from Bastar's bell-metal and mural heritage.

## Cultural Significance

Chhattisgarh's Kosa silk occupies an enduring place among connoisseurs who value sculptural minimalism and honest raw-fiber luster over heavy sequined ornamentation—suited equally for formal receptions, cultural gatherings, and daytime ceremonies.

## Care & Preservation Guidance

- Always entrust pure Kosa and Ghicha silk sarees to experienced dry cleaners; never soak or wring wet silk.
- Spot-blotting water spills immediately from beneath with a clean dry cloth prevents faint tide marks.
- Keep perfumes and aerosol sprays away from undyed natural protein fibers.
- Store flat inside soft cotton or muslin wraps in a cool, dry cupboard.
