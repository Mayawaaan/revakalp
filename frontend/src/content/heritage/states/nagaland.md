## History & Origin

Across Nagaland's distinct Ao, Angami, Chakhesang, Sumi, and Lotha valleys, handweaving carries profound civic memory—historically recording community achievements, clan ancestry, and seasonal wisdom inside fearless geometric stripes and stylized spears woven exclusively by women on portable backstrap **loin looms**.

Today, weaver collectives across **Kohima, Dimapur, and Phek** adapt these sacred graphic grammars onto structured contemporary cotton and Eri-silk six-yard sarees with unmistakable sculptural strength.

## Regional Craft & Weaving Centers

**Kohima**, **Phek** (famous for intricate Chakhesang *Eri* and wild nettle fibers), and **Mokokchung** (heartland of bold Ao red-and-black bands) nurture Nagaland's handloom artistry, balancing earthy madder reds, deep indigo blacks, and chalk-ivory stripes accented with miniature geometric inlays.

## Material Characteristics & Drape

Dense warp-faced weaves grant Naga cottons and Eri-silk drapes remarkable architectural weight and durability—settling in clean graphic panels across the shoulder while framing bold contrasts cleanly down the border.

## Craftsmanship & Loom Technique

On a traditional bamboo **loin loom** anchored between a sturdy wall beam and a belt wrapped around the weaver's waist, human posture literally tensions every pick. Artisans insert bold crimson and bone supplementary geometric strips directly inside obsidian grounds by hand-counting picks.

## Cultural Significance

Seen gloriously during the **Hornbill Festival** and worn confidently as minimalist art textiles across modern India, Nagaland's crimson-and-black weaves celebrate fierce dignity, community craft, and graphic honesty.

## Care & Preservation Guidance

- Gently dry-clean or cold hand-wash contrasting crimson-and-ivory cotton drapes separately so saturated dyes stay crisp without tinting pale stripes.
- Air dry flat in cool shade; avoid twisting or machine tumble-drying dense warp-faced cotton.
- Steam or iron on reverse while slightly damp.
- Keep neatly folded in dry cotton sleeves.
