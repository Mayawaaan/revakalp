## History & Origin

Named after ancient Pratishthana—modern Paithan along the Godavari River—Paithani grew from a centuries-old Deccan trading capital into the crowning silk pride of Maharashtra during the 17th- and 18th-century Peshwa era.

Early chroniclers praised its liquid gold pallus and *dhoop-chhaon* dual-color iridescence, woven by changing warp and weft dye tones so the silk shifts between ruby, magenta, plum, and peacock green as the wearer moves.

## Regional Craft & Weaving Centers

Today, both Paithan in Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar district and Yeola in Nashik district anchor traditional Paithani weaving—distinguished by motifs like *Bangadi Mor* (peacocks inside a bangle), *Muniya* (green parrots), *Akruti* almond buds, and sacred lotus blossoms (*kamal*).

Traditional Deccan palettes glow with magenta (*rani*), peacock teal (*morpankhi*), deep purple (*jambli*), pearl white (*motiya*), and pomegranate red anchored by broad gleaming gold borders.

## Material Characteristics & Drape

Untwisted and finely twisted mulberry yarns give Paithani a remarkably supple, fluid drape—lighter at the waist than heavier southern brocades yet richly illuminated across the trailing shoulder pallu.

Because the pallu motifs are integrated directly into the ground warp rather than stitched on top, the finished panel drapes effortlessly in luminous metallic sheets.

## Craftsmanship & Loom Technique

Unlike mechanical jacquard brocades that leave loose floating threads behind the cloth, classic Paithani employs a free-hand **tapestry technique** using dozens of tiny bamboo or reed bobbins (*tilli*) wound with brightly colored silk.

Weavers interlock each tiny motif color directly around the warp threads by finger count—meaning a traditional handwoven pallu displays an almost identical, crisp picture on both obverse and reverse sides without messy hanging floats.

## Cultural Significance

Cherished across Maharashtrian bridal trousseaus and festive gatherings in neighboring Malwa as well, a Paithani saree embodies enduring celebration, family blessing, and lyrical craftsmanship handed gently across generations.

## Care & Preservation Guidance

- Entrust Paithani silk sarees exclusively to reputable specialist dry cleaners.
- Cushion the heavy tapestry pallu with soft acid-free tissue paper or unbleached cotton cloth when folding.
- Keep bracelets, wristwatch clasps, and brooch pins away from interlocked tapestry threads while draping.
- Store inside breathable cotton bags without naphthalene mothballs.
