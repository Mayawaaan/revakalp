## History & Origin

Across the misty **Ri-Bhoi** and Khasi-Jaintia hills of Meghalaya, **Ryndia** (*Eri* silk, *Samia ricini*) is cherished not only as heirloom dress (*Dhara* and *Jainsen*) but as a living ecological bond—since the silkworm emerges safely as a moth from its open cocoon before the soft staple fibers are hand-spun (*Ahimsa* / peace silk).

Every Khasi hill cluster from **Umden-Diwon** to Jaintia villages grew castor plants beside rice fields and dyed handspun skeins in deep wild forest extracts—lac insects for ruby crimson, turmeric for gold, and natural tree barks for warm slate.

## Regional Craft & Weaving Centers

**Umden and Diwon** villages in Ri-Bhoi district stand celebrated as Meghalaya's Ryndia weaving heartland—producing luminous unbleached ivory, warm walnut, lac crimson, and indigo *Eri* sarees alongside fine **Garo Dakmanda** diamond geometric borders from western Meghalaya.

## Material Characteristics & Drape

Unlike slippery continuous mulberry filaments, **Eri peace silk** is spun like fine cotton or cashmere—yielding an ultra-soft, matte-luster fabric with subtle thermal intelligence that cools during sultry afternoons and warms comfortably during misty hill evenings.

Over years of wear, Ryndia grows softer without losing structural resilience along its pleats.

## Craftsmanship & Loom Technique

Because Eri cocoons have open ends, they cannot be reeled in one continuous thread—artisans gently degum cocoons in wood ash or soap nut, pull soft clouds of silk by hand over wooden spindles (*takli*), and beat dense plain or basket weaves interlaced with *extra-weft* Khasi flower diamonds along the pallu.

## Cultural Significance

Essential across Shad Suk Mynsiem spring dances, Nongkrem ceremonies, and respectful milestone gifting throughout Meghalaya, a Ryndia Eri saree embodies compassionate slow-craft harmony from cocoon to loom.

## Care & Preservation Guidance

- Gently hand-wash plain unbleached Ryndia Eri sarees in cool water using mild organic pH-neutral soap; dry-clean richly dyed lac/indigo bordered drapes.
- Press gently flat inside a dry cotton towel—never hang heavy wet Eri silk as warm water stretches handspun yarn.
- Air dry evenly in cool hill-like shade away from blazing afternoon sun.
- Store folded cleanly in cotton cloth between wears.
