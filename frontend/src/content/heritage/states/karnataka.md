## History & Origin

Karnataka supplies the majority of India's raw mulberry silk, and under royal Wodeyar patronage in the early 20th century, **Mysuru** transformed clean, untextured high-twist silk into sleek monochrome drapes edged simply in radiant gold—setting an aesthetic standard for poised minimalism.

Across northern Karnataka in **Ilkal** (Bagalkot district), handloom weaving flourished since the 8th century alongside **Kasuti**—a poetic, counted-thread folk embroidery worked patiently by women without tracing paper or knots.

## Regional Craft & Weaving Centers

**Mysuru and Ramanagara** stand at the heart of smooth high-twist silk, while **Ilkal**, **Molakalmuru** (Chitradurga), and **Guledgudda** preserve bold contrasting borders, red-bordered striped pallus, and rich *Kasuti* motifs showing stylized *tulasi* pots, temple *gopurams*, and elephants.

## Material Characteristics & Drape

Classic **Mysore Crepe Silk** possesses a fluid, cool weightlessness that hugs pleats closely without stiff volume—gliding smoothly with a liquid satin shimmer across royal burgundy, bottle green, and peacock teal shades.

By contrast, **Ilkal** drapes blend breathable cotton bodies with lustrous silk borders and striking *tope teni* pallus edged in deep crimson and cream stripes.

## Craftsmanship & Loom Technique

In Mysore silk weaving, silk yarns receive opposing high twists before weaving and careful degumming afterward, creating an elastic, ripple-smooth satin drape framed by sleek zari borders (*kasuri*, checks, or delicate mango paisleys).

Authentic **Ilkal** sarees utilize the **Tope Teni** loop join—linking a crimson silk pallu warp to the body cotton warp by hand loops before shuttles lock the transition permanently.

## Cultural Significance

Beloved across Karnataka for quiet elegance during weddings, festivals, and formal evenings, Mysore Silk remains synonymous with effortless poise, while Ilkal and Kasuti bring soulful regional folklore to everyday festive wear.

## Care & Preservation Guidance

- Dry-clean pure Mysore Crepe Silk carefully; water droplets can temporarily leave matte spots on high-twist crepe until pressed properly.
- Gently dry-clean silk-bordered Ilkal sarees and embroidered Kasuti pieces rather than wringing.
- Store Mysore silk folded smoothly in dark cotton bags to protect deep natural and mineral shades.
- Iron only on low silk setting from the reverse side using a dry cotton pressing sheet.
