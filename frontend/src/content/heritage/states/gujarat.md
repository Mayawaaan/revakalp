## History & Origin

In 12th-century Solanki Gujarat, Patan blossomed into a premier center for *Patola* ("queen of silks")—an astonishing double-ikat discipline so cherished for auspicious purity that its textiles traveled from western Indian ports across maritime routes to Southeast Asia.

Parallel to Patan's geometric looms, Gujarat's Kutch and Saurashtra coastlines cultivated India's finest satin *Gajji* silk Bandhani, shimmering *gharchola* wedding grids, and mirror-bright pastoral embroidery.

## Regional Craft & Weaving Centers

Patan in northern Gujarat remains iconic for authentic **double-ikat Patola**, while Rajkot and Surendranagar produce expressive single-ikat warp or weft Patola styles. Farther west in Bhuj, Bhujodi, and Jamnagar, artisans tie thousands of microscopic *rai-bandhej* mustard-seed knots over glossy satin-weave *gajji* silk.

Colors carry joyful warmth: vermilion crimson, marigold yellow, deep emerald, indigo, and soft ivory framed cleanly by multi-hued striped borders.

## Material Characteristics & Drape

Patan and Rajkot silk Patola drapes with crisp precision and zero surface relief—each diamond, lotus, and elephant pixel exists structurally inside the intersection of warp and weft rather than stamped onto the face.

By contrast, Gujarati *Gajji* satin silk brings a fluid, luminous sheen with gentle texture wherever tiny resist knots once sat.

## Craftsmanship & Loom Technique

Double-ikat Patola is one of textile art's most demanding mathematical achievements. Both the vertical **warp** threads and horizontal **weft** threads are grouped, calculated to the millimeter, bound with resist cotton cord, and dyed sequentially color by color *before weaving begins*.

On the inclined wooden loom, weavers align every single weft pick with iron needles so pre-dyed horizontal and vertical sections intersect to form razor-sharp stepped borders—identical on both front and back.

## Cultural Significance

From bridal *Panetar* and auspicious *Gharchola* drapes to heirloom *Navratna* Patola, Gujarat's textiles express clarity, geometry, and family prosperity at the heart of wedding wardrobes.

## Care & Preservation Guidance

- Dry-clean Patola and Gajji Bandhani silk strictly through trusted silk specialists.
- Keep Patola wrapped cleanly in soft unbleached cotton cloth away from bright UV exposure.
- Steam Gajji Bandhani gently from a few inches away to preserve natural drape and color richness.
- Avoid folding along identical sharp creases year after year.
