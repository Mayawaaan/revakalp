## History & Origin

Varanasi's reputation as a textile capital stretches back over two millennia, with Buddhist and classical texts mentioning gleaming *Kashi* silks traded across caravan routes. During the Mughal period and 19th-century Awadhi courts, local master *karigars* synthesized Persian floral latticework with Indian ceremonial motifs into the iconic Banarasi brocade language.

From heavy ceremonial *Katan* silk to crisp summer *Kora* organza and soft *Georgette* drapes, Uttar Pradesh's weaving lanes built one of the world's most versatile brocade traditions.

## Regional Craft & Weaving Centers

Varanasi's Madanpura, Alaipura, and Lohta quarters anchor pure silk brocades, supported by rich weaving towns across Mubarakpur (Azamgarh), Mau, and Bhadohi in eastern Uttar Pradesh. Each neighborhood specializes in distinct weaves: deep-lustre *Katan*, silk-by-cotton *Shattir*, multi-colored *Meenakari*, and seamless satin *Tanchoi*.

Master designers (*nakshabands*) first sketch the scrolling vines and paisley arches on graph paper (*naksha*), dictating the rhythm of every metallic thread lift.

## Material Characteristics & Drape

Classic Katan Banarasi weaves two twisted mulberry threads into a lustrous, crisp plain or satin ground that supports intricate metallic patterning while holding clean pleats. Lighter *Georgette* and *Kora Tissue* variations soften the fall for agile daytime or cocktail wear.

Genuine *Meenakari* accents add jewel-like ruby, emerald, or sapphire silk dots nestled directly inside gold brocade petals without thickening the cloth.

## Craftsmanship & Loom Technique

In prized **Kadwa** weaving, artisans work each motif individually with miniature shuttles so zero loose weft floats cross the reverse side—creating crisp silhouettes on delicate *Kora* and *Katan* alike.

In **Cutwork** (*katua*) and **Fekwa** weaves, extra-weft threads span between motifs and are trimmed cleanly on the back, while **Tanchoi** embeds soft self-colored satin brocade straight into the silk without any metallic zari at all.

## Cultural Significance

Across every corner of India, a crimson, deep wine, or ivory-gold Banarasi remains an undisputed bridal classic—carried from engagement rituals and wedding trousseaus to milestone anniversaries for generations.

## Care & Preservation Guidance

- Dry-clean Banarasi Katan, Organza, and Tissue sarees exclusively using reputable silk care specialists.
- Refold sarees along different crease lines every 3 months with clean acid-free tissue inside gold brocade folds.
- Wrap in breathable soft cotton cloth; avoid plastic garment covers that trap humidity and dull metallic zari.
- Never apply hot steam directly over meenakari or tissue threads.
