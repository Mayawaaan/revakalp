## History & Origin

Across eastern Punjab and modern Haryana, **Panipat** long held fame as a disciplined textile hub for handspun cotton, heavy woven *Khes* geometrics, and rich natural indigo and madder dyeing—serving farming households and trading caravans across the Yamuna plains.

Alongside sturdy loom craft, rural women across **Rohtak, Jind, and Hisar** stitched ceremonial **Chope** and **Subhar** drapes marked by balanced two-sided triangular borders and sparse *Til-Patra* ("sesame-seed") embroidery reserved for weddings and Teej gatherings.

## Regional Craft & Weaving Centers

**Panipat** continues as northern India's powerhouse of durable handloom texture—producing breathable slubbed cottons, khadi-silk blends, and geometric *dobby* borders, while artisans in **Rohtak and Rewari** preserve bright mustard, indigo, and pomegranate festive borders accented with fine geometric stitchwork.

## Material Characteristics & Drape

Haryana's cotton-silk and fine *khaddar*-inspired drapes balance cool airiness with crisp structured pleats—ideal for sun-lit daytime ceremonies and all-season wear.

When paired with soft handspun borders in terracotta, mustard gold, or deep maroon, the fabric carries an honest tactile grain that softens with every wear.

## Craftsmanship & Loom Technique

Drawing directly from Haryana's traditional **Khes** loom heritage, border harnesses weave rhythmic diamond checks and stepped borders into tight cotton-silk warps.

Meanwhile, **Chope** border work employs crisp double-sided Holbein-like running stitches so triangular edges appear identically crisp on both inside and outside turns of the pallu.

## Cultural Significance

From sunlit Haryanvi Teej swings to joyous rural wedding rituals, these understated geometric cottons and red-edged Chope weaves honor craftsmanship forged patiently in everyday household looms.

## Care & Preservation Guidance

- Gently hand-wash pure cotton Panipat-style sarees in cool water with mild soap; dry-clean silk-blended or embroidered Chope pieces.
- Dry shaded away from direct harsh sunlight to preserve warm terracotta and turmeric tones.
- Lightly press cotton pleats while faintly damp with medium heat, protecting stitched borders from the reverse.
- Store inside clean breathable cotton wraps.
