## History & Origin

Along the Deccan plateau, Telangana developed an extraordinary contrast between ultra-crisp silk-cotton engineering and hypnotic tie-dyed geometry. Originally woven as cotton turbans (*telia rumal*) treated with oil and natural madder dyes across Chirala and Nalgonda, ikat techniques found a thriving modern sanctuary in **Bhoodan Pochampally**, transforming into beloved silk and cotton sarees by the mid-20th century.

Farther south along the Krishna and Tungabhadra rivers, **Gadwal** artisans perfected light cotton or silk-cotton sarees joined to grand, opulent pure-silk borders—originally famous for folding small enough to fit inside a matchbox.

## Regional Craft & Weaving Centers

**Bhoodan Pochampally, Koyyalagudem, and Puttapaka** in Yadadri Bhuvanagiri district form the celebrated "Silk City" ikat belt of Telangana. Meanwhile, **Gadwal** in Jogulamba Gadwal district and historic **Narayanpet** nurture rich contrast-border temple sarees beloved across South and Central India.

## Material Characteristics & Drape

Pochampally silk ikat wears with lively, buoyant grace—ideal for all-day gatherings where bold stepped colors bring unmistakable contemporary energy.

Gadwal *Sico* (silk-cotton) and pure *Katan Gadwal* sarees delight wearers seeking breezy waist comfort balanced against rich golden borders that stay anchored neatly around the ankles.

## Craftsmanship & Loom Technique

For Pochampally ikat, weavers wind yarn in circular fans (*asu*) before bundling and tightly knotting rubber bindings to resist dye baths—yielding crisp geometric stars, honeycombs, and chevron diamonds.

In **Gadwal**, the signature **kuttu** join links a featherlight body warp with rich silk-and-zari border warps using three shuttles so the transition seam feels smooth and indestructible.

## Cultural Significance

From bright Pochampally ikats favored for festivals, gifting, and cultural evenings to sacred Gadwal weaves worn at weddings and pujas, Telangana offers remarkable range between graphic modernity and temple heritage.

## Care & Preservation Guidance

- Dry-clean Pochampally silk and contrast-border Gadwal sarees—especially *Sico* pieces combining cotton bodies with pure zari silk borders, as uneven home washing can shrink the two fibers differently.
- Store inside dry breathable cotton sleeves away from direct sunlight.
- Press softly on reverse using low silk heat without pressing directly across heavy zari ridges.
- Refold every few months along shifted lines.
