## History & Origin

At elevations above 14,000 feet on Ladakh's cold Changthang plateau, hardy goats grow an ultra-fine winter undercoat known in Persian as *pashm* ("soft gold"). For centuries, this prized fleece traveled down mountain passes into the Kashmir Valley, where artisans spun and wove it into winter shawls and ceremonial drapes celebrated from Mughal courts to distant capitals.

Over the last century, Kashmir's shawl grammar expanded beautifully into full six-yard winter sarees—pairing featherlight insulation with timeless almond (*badam*), tulip, and maple (*chinar*) ornamentation.

## Regional Craft & Weaving Centers

While Changthang herders gather the raw mountain fleece during spring molting, downtown Srinagar, Kanihama, and Ganderbal serve as the historic ateliers where fleece is hand-combed, spun on wooden *yender* wheels, woven on pit looms, and finished with Sozni needlework.

Kani weavers in Kanihama still translate coded script patterns (*talim*) row by row using miniature notched wooden spools (*tujis*), building intricate floral borders without sewing a single stitch afterwards.

## Material Characteristics & Drape

True Pashmina filaments measure just 12 to 15 microns wide—far finer than ordinary sheep wool—giving pure Pashmina and silk-Pashmina blends an almost weightless, buttery softness against skin.

Rather than stiffly standing away from the figure, a 2/2 twill or silk-warp Pashmina winter saree settles smoothly into slender pleats while blocking chilly evening breezes effortlessly.

## Craftsmanship & Loom Technique

Classic winter sarees feature either subtle *Chashme Bulbul* ("nightingale's eye") diamond twill textures across walnut, ivory, or burgundy grounds, or intricate **Kani** tapestry borders and pallus where wooden spools interlock dyed yarns thread by thread.

Other pieces are finished with fine **Sozni** satin-stitch embroidery around the border (*hashiya*) and pallu corners, rendering delicate paisley vines and iris clusters with needlework nearly invisible from the back.

## Cultural Significance

Whenever crisp winter receptions, late-evening pheras, or Lohri-to-Basant festivities arrive across North and Central India, a Kashmiri Pashmina or Kani winter saree grants rare thermal ease wrapped in quiet aristocratic grace.

## Care & Preservation Guidance

- Dry-clean Pashmina and Kani garments lightly only when needed using gentle organic hydrocarbon solvents.
- Always store folded flat rather than hanging so soft protein fibers never stretch under their own weight.
- Wrap in clean breathable cotton cloth alongside mild natural cedarwood or dried neem leaves between winters.
- Refresh creases gently using indirect mist or low-heat steam through a clean damp muslin cloth.
