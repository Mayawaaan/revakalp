## History & Origin

Woven in the ancient temple town of Kanchipuram near Chennai, Kanjivaram silk developed alongside Chola and Vijayanagara temple patronage—drawing both its rich mineral palette and sculptural motif grammar from granite shrines, bronze lamps, and carved temple towers.

Over generations, traditional weaving communities mastered the combination of sturdy Karnataka mulberry filaments with luminous silver-and-gold electroplated zari threads to produce drapes prized as family heirlooms.

## Regional Craft & Weaving Centers

Kanchipuram stands at the heart of Tamil Nadu's heavy silk heritage, supported by allied silk clusters in Arni, Thirubuvanam, and Kumbakonam. Borders frequently contrast starkly with the body—vermilion meeting peacock blue, bottle green meeting turmeric yellow, or royal burgundy paired with antique gold.

Along the body, classic *kattam* checks, *vari* hairline stripes, or scattered floral *buttas* frame broad borders crowned with *gopuram* temple triangles and mythological *yali* or *annapakshi* mythical birds.

## Material Characteristics & Drape

Authentic Kanjivarams use thick three-ply twisted mulberry yarns in both warp and weft. This heavy density creates a liquid yet structural drape—falling into deep, architectural pleats that stay crisp through marathon wedding ceremonies without crushing.

Under warm indoor chandeliers or natural temple light, the dense silk rib reflects rich dual-tone depth while heavy zari borders anchor the hemline seamlessly.

## Craftsmanship & Loom Technique

Contrast-border Kanjivarams rely on the painstaking **korvai** three-shuttle technique, requiring two weavers working in tandem on the same loom—one throwing the central body shuttle while the assistant locks separate left and right border shuttles at every pick so the contrasting seam is structurally unbreakable.

For grand *petni* pallus where the entire trailing panel shifts to a different shade from the body, warp threads are individually spliced and hand-joined before weaving resumes.

## Cultural Significance

Whether chosen for Muhurtham mornings in South India or grand evening receptions in Indore, a Kanjivaram represents enduring heirloom substance—often preserved carefully across daughters and daughters-in-law for decades.

## Care & Preservation Guidance

- Dry-clean Kanjivaram sarees strictly through trusted silk specialists; never machine-wash or soak in water.
- Store inside clean, soft cotton or breathable muslin covers rather than synthetic plastic pouches.
- Refold neatly every 3 to 4 months along alternate lines so zari threads along creases never fatigue.
- Avoid hanging heavy silk on thin metal hangers; always store flat away from sunlight.
