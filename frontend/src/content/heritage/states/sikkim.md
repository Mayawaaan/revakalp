## History & Origin

Nestled beneath the glaciers of Kanchenjunga, **Sikkim's** textile personality weaves together two highland threads: the indigenous **Lepcha** handloom (*Thokro-Dum* striped wraps crafted along **Dzongu's** steep forested valleys) and rich **Bhutia/Sikkimese** silk brocades bearing auspicious Himalayan monastery symbols (*Ashtamangala*—endless knots, lotuses, and rhythmic cloud scrolls).

Translated gracefully into contemporary winter sarees and striped celebratory wraps across Gangtok and Namchi ateliers, Sikkim's aesthetic exudes alpine calm and celebratory clarity.

## Regional Craft & Weaving Centers

**Dzongu** in North Sikkim safeguards heritage striped Lepcha weaves tinted with forest madder, turmeric, and wild bark, while **Gangtok and Namchi** craft delicate silk-blend winter drapes trimmed with bright gold brocade strips and traditional *Kho/Bakhu* silk palettes.

## Material Characteristics & Drape

Sikkim's cotton-wool and silk-blended striped drapes maintain pleasing cozy body warmth while remaining surprisingly light and agile around waist pleats—allowing crisp contrasting stripe rhythms to elongate the silhouette gracefully.

## Craftsmanship & Loom Technique

On rustic bamboo loin looms and wider village handlooms, artisans warp finely graded rainbow ribbons of forest green, marigold, burgundy, sky blue, and bone ivory alongside diamond **endless-knot** extra-weft inserts at the pallu edge.

## Cultural Significance

Radiant during *Losar*, *Pang Lhabsol*, and serene mountain wedding celebrations, Sikkim's crisp striped textiles evoke sacred peaks, monastic harmony, and timeless alpine warmth.

## Care & Preservation Guidance

- Dry-clean silk-brocade bordered sarees and delicate natural-dyed Dzongu weaves gently.
- Air wool-cotton stripes flat in cool dry mountain-like shade rather than direct midday sun.
- Press cautiously from the back over clean cotton pressing cloth.
- Keep neatly wrapped inside breathable cotton sleeves.
