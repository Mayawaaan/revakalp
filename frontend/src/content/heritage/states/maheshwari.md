## History & Origin

In the late 18th century, Rani Ahilyabai Holkar encouraged master weavers to settle in the sandstone fort town of Maheshwar along the Narmada River. Her aim was to create a royal gift weave light enough for Central Indian summers yet dignified for Maratha court occasions.

Alongside Chanderi farther north, Madhya Pradesh cultivated a distinct Malwa aesthetic focused on translucent weightlessness, disciplined geometry, and restrained metallic accents drawn directly from carved temple balconies and fort battlements.

## Regional Craft & Weaving Centers

Maheshwar in Khargone district sits barely two hours south of Revakalp in Indore. Weavers here draw inspiration from the Ahilya Fort ghats, Narmada river ripples (*Narmada Leher*), and the stone-carved *mehraab* arches framing the riverbank.

Chanderi in Ashoknagar district complements this tradition with ultra-sheer mulberry silk warps and crisp *butti* motifs—coins (*asharfi*), floral sprigs, and peacocks—woven cleanly into pastel and jewel-toned grounds.

## Material Characteristics & Drape

Classic Maheshwari sarees pair a fine mulberry silk warp with a breathable mercerized cotton weft. This balanced construction lends a subtle crispness at the waist pleats while allowing cool air circulation during daytime ceremonies.

When finished in tissue or silk-by-silk variations, the pallu catches warm sunlight softly through alternating matte cotton bands and delicate *zari* stripes, creating five neat *panch-rangi* bands at the trailing end.

## Craftsmanship & Loom Technique

The signature technical hallmark of a Maheshwari saree is its reversible border, known locally as *bugdi*. Supplementary warp and weft tension is balanced so meticulously on the pit loom that the geometric border reads cleanly from both sides with minimal loose threads.

Fields traditionally feature plain jewel solids, subtle horizontal *baingani* (plum) or deep maroon stripes, or crisp checks (*chatai*), keeping visual weight balanced between border and body.

## Cultural Significance

Across Indore, Ujjain, Bhopal, and throughout India, Maheshwari and Chanderi sarees remain staple wardrobe choices for morning pujas, milestone family visits, and summer weddings where comfort and effortless poise matter equally.

## Care & Preservation Guidance

- Dry-clean silk and cotton-silk Maheshwari and Chanderi sarees, especially deep shades or pieces with zari stripes.
- Wrap folded sarees inside breathable unbleached cotton cloth rather than humid plastic sleeves.
- Refold along slightly shifted crease lines every few months so metallic border threads stay supple.
- Press gently from the reverse side beneath a thin cotton pressing cloth on low silk heat.
