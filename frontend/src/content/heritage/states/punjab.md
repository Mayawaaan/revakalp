## History & Origin

Literally meaning "flower work" (*phul* + *kari*), Punjab's iconic embroidery blossomed historically when grandmothers and mothers prepared *Chope* and *Bagh* wedding odhnis over years of tender needlework—pouring joyous marigold, sindoor crimson, and emerald silk floss onto rustic brick-red handspun cloth (*khaddar*).

Today, Patiala and Amritsar artisans translate those very geometric stitches onto flowing pure crepe silk, chinon, organza, and tussar sarees—bringing Punjab's celebratory needlework into versatile six-yard silhouettes.

## Regional Craft & Weaving Centers

**Patiala, Nabha, and Tripuri** anchor classical Phulkari ateliers, while **Amritsar and Jalandhar** sustain vibrant bridal border and pallu craft—preserving motifs like *Chashma-e-Bulbul*, *Surajmukhi* sunflowers, *Karela* waves, and gleaming *Sheesha* mirror sparkles.

## Material Characteristics & Drape

Untwisted silk embroidery yarn (**pat**) lies flat across the cloth, creating intense jewel-like shine that catches warm light smoothly from alternate angles depending on stitch direction.

When embroidered onto lightweight silk georgette or tussar crepe rather than heavy khaddar, the finished saree flows cleanly while keeping crisp sculptural weight along the pallu.

## Craftsmanship & Loom Technique

Unlike freehand chain stitching, classic Phulkari relies strictly on **counted-thread darning stitches worked from the reverse side** without penciled stencils or embroidery hoops.

Each geometric petal or chevron emerges strictly by counting warp and weft threads underneath—resulting in mirror-bright *Bagh* surfaces where every thread of the base cloth hides beneath a carpet of luminous silk stitches.

## Cultural Significance

Integral to Lohri bonfires, Baisakhi harvests, mehendi mornings, and Anand Karaj celebrations across northern India, a Phulkari-draped saree radiates unmistakable Punjabi warmth, blessing, and joyful abundance.

## Care & Preservation Guidance

- Always dry-clean Phulkari silk, chinon, and tussar sarees; home washing can swell untwisted silk *pat* threads and pucker light bases.
- Fold carefully with soft unbleached muslin over silk-floss clusters so bangles, zippers, or clasps never snag floating surface stitches.
- Iron strictly from the reverse face over a cushioned soft towel on low silk warmth.
- Store flat inside breathable cotton wraps away from humidity.
